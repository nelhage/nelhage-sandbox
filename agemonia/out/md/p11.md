#### Reactive Ability Checks [Reaction Die: Blank] (p7)

> **Sidebar — Each Ally has 3 abilities:**
>
> - [Might] Might
> - [Agility] Agility
> - [Will] Will

A reactive ability check is signified by a number in a blue box followed by one of the 3 ability symbols.

Some reactive ability checks allow you to choose between two or more abilities. In this case, the ability symbols are separated by a '|'.

Reactive ability checks are performed by rolling a number of Reaction (blue) dice equal to your Ally's level in the given ability.

> **Reaction dice**
>
> There are various symbols on a Reaction die:
>
> - [Reaction Die: Blank] 0 successes
> - [Reaction Die: 1 Shield] 1 success
> - [Reaction Die: 2 Shields] 2 successes
> - [Reaction Die: Reaction] May be used to activate an effect your Ally has.
>   If your Ally has no such effects, it counts as 0 successes.

> **Sidebar — Example:** [Damage] 3 [Might]
>
> *The Ally will suffer 3 [Damage] damage, reduced by the number of [Shield] they roll on a reactive might check.*
>
> **Sidebar — Example:** [Stamina] 4 [Might]
>
> *The Ally will lose 4 [Stamina], reduced by the number of [Shield] they roll on a reactive might check.*

##### Reactive Ability Check to Reduce Damage [Damage] or Stamina [Stamina] Loss (p7)

The most common use of a reactive ability check is to reduce damage. These checks show the damage symbol [Damage] before the check. Some attacks and other effects cause stamina loss. These checks show the stamina symbol [Stamina] before the check.

In either case, for each [Shield] you roll, the damage or stamina loss is reduced by 1.

Remember that Non-Hero Allies treat stamina loss as damage.

### Items (p7)

During the game your Hero will gain items in the form of Item cards and Item tokens. See p. 45 for an explanation of the various item effects.

> **Sidebar — Item card example:**
>
> *Card: an Item card, "Cube of Annihilation," with a sale-value coin reading 5, and an effects box below showing a passive icon and an active-effect row; the individual icons and numbers in that row are rasterised too coarsely to read.*
>
> **Sidebar — Item token example:**
>
> *Token: a blue-grey disc with a white and orange starburst emblem.*

##### Gaining Item Cards (p7)

When your Hero gains an Item card, you will be told the number and name of that Item card. Find the relevant Item card using the number. Look for the Item card in the Stock first and then the Archive. When looking through the Archive, be careful not to see the front of any other Item cards.

If it is not in the Stock or the Archive (because all copies of it are already in use), instead gain an amount of Star equal to the sale price of the Item card.

##### Gaining Item Tokens (p7)

When your Hero gains an Item token, you will be told the name and shown an image of the Item token.

If it is not in the Stock (because all copies of it are already in use), instead gain an amount of Star equal to the sale price of the Item token.

Use the same approach if you are instructed to place an unavailable Item token on a Story card; place an amount of Star equal to the sale price of the Item token on the Story card instead.

##### Taking from the Stock (p7)

You may sometimes be told to take an item from the Stock. In this case, if the item is not in the Stock you may not take it from the Archive (in the case of an Item card) and do not gain Star equal to its sale price instead.

### [Star] Stars (p7)

Stars are the currency used in Agemonia and are represented by the [Star] symbol. Only the total value you have matters, so feel free to exchange coins for others of the same total value whenever you like.

*Illustrations: the six Star coin/token denominations — 1 (bronze, unlabelled), 3 (silver triangle), 5 (purple pentagon), 10 (gold circle), 20 (blue square), and 50 (green pentagon).*
